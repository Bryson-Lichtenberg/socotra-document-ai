# Rule routing audit (Workstream 3, research only)

Status: audit of the rules that already exist. No implementation code was changed. The machine-readable version is
[`research/rule-routing-audit.json`](../research/rule-routing-audit.json), generated by [`research/build_rule_routing_audit.py`](../research/build_rule_routing_audit.py), which copies provenance, current labels
and the last traceability verdicts from the existing artifacts and adds the hand classification.

Inputs audited: the 17 Florida rules in `sample/rules/regulatory/fl-declarations-rules.approved.json` (with review
decisions and the 150 triaged checklist rows), the three synthetic rules in `sample/rules/selection-rules.approved.json`,
the rule models (`models/regulatory.py`, `models/rules.py`), and the code that consumes them (`rules/traceability.py`,
`rules/applicability.py`, `rules/evaluate.py`, `rules/regulatory.py`). The NFIP rule was modelled by hand from the
October 2025 Flood Insurance Manual.

## Short answer

The class / sink / obligation decomposition describes the evidence cleanly, with one missing pair: requirements to
*offer* something (a $500 AOP deductible, replacement cost, a windstorm exclusion) belong to product configuration, and
none of the six sinks fits them.

The current schema does not route by class at all. `rule_type` is never read by the code. The only router is
`requirement.document`: declarations targets are traced against the rendered page, and everything else is marked out of
scope and validated nowhere. That produces three kinds of error:

- **Compound rules lose their non-template half.** Four approved declarations rules are really "compute, then display".
  The display half is checked; the calculation half has no owner and no check.
- **Approved rules with a non-declarations target disappear.** The law and ordinance statement is approved, targeted
  at `policy_form`, and so is never checked anywhere.
- **Labels are wrong where the routing happens to be right.** Four rules are labelled `presentation` because
  "prescribed statement" and "execution workflow" aren't `rule_type` values.

One schema can hold all the classes if the class and the routes are stored separately from the target document, and if
a rule can carry more than one route, each with its own approval. That is a small, additive change (question 2).

## How the code routes today

- `check_document()` in `traceability.py` switches on `requirement.document` and `requirement.kind`. In-scope documents
  are `declarations`, `declarations_or_premium_notice`, `declarations_or_renewal_notice` and `page_after_declarations`;
  anything else is `out_of_scope`.
- `presentation_hints()` feeds the template generator from rules that are approved, have kind `field_value`, have an
  in-scope document, and carry presentation flags. It ignores `rule_type` and `applies_when`.
- `approved` is global. In practice it means "approved for the declarations page". The four rules rejected in review
  aren't wrong; they're about other documents. Nothing distinguishes "wrong" from "not ours".
- `applies_when` mixes two scopes. Product-level predicates (jurisdiction, product family, policy type) decide whether
  a rule applies to a resource at all. Policy-level predicates (flood provided, separate hurricane deductible) have to
  become template conditionals or selection conditions at runtime. Today both are evaluated only by traceability,
  against one sample policy.
- Only rows triaged declarations-relevant were structured into rules. None of these were stored as rules:
  - 16 underwriting/eligibility rows (cancellation and nonrenewal restrictions)
  - 9 filing-process rows
  - 35 claims-process rows

  They aren't misrouted to the template; they're routed nowhere.

- **correct:** right class and sink.
- **mislabeled:** right sink, wrong `rule_type`.
- **misrouted:** the current target points at the wrong sink.
- **partially routed:** one obligation reaches a sink and another reaches none.
- **unrouted:** correctly kept off the declarations page, but nothing receives the rule.

### Rules at risk of being treated as declarations or template requirements

These are the cases the brief asked to flag: rules that look like "put something on the declarations page" but are
really something else.

- **HURRICANE_DEDUCTIBLE_DISPLAY: calculation.** "Must be computed and prominently displayed." The prominence hint
  works. The dollar value is computed by a Workstream 2 mapping transform that no rule references, including the
  inflation-guard adjustment in 627.701(4)(c).
- **ROOF_DEDUCTIBLE_DISPLAY: calculation, product configuration, and a hidden second rule.**
  - Applicability is unknown because the product has no roof deductible. That is a product question.
  - The statute excerpt stored as evidence is actually 627.701(4)(e)1, not the cited (e)2. (e)1 requires a prescribed
    bold 18pt statement alone on the page immediately behind the declarations page. That is a different rule, it has
    no rule of its own, and nothing detects the mismatch.
  - The related 2%-or-50% cap (checklist row p13-r6) is a product calculation constraint and was never structured.
- **LEGISLATIVE_DISCOUNTS: calculation, and a false "present".** 624.5108(1) says the insurer "must deduct" specific
  amounts; that's rating.
  - The rule's only field is the whole discounts collection. The candidate check reports "present" because of
    "Proof of Updates - Roof Only" and "Windstorm Loss Mitigation", neither of which is a legislative discount.
  - `value_correctness` can't be expressed without the expected item names.
- **HURRICANE_PREMIUM_SEPARATE: depends on a calculation.** The split is rating output. It's labelled `presentation`,
  but the obligation is separation, not prominence. It passes; nothing checks the amounts against rating.
- **AGENT_SIGNATURE: execution workflow.** Countersignature is a carrier process. Traceability returns REVIEW, which
  is the right outcome for the wrong reason (text_mention has no mechanical check).
- **PHONE_NUMBER_NOTICE and LAW_AND_ORDINANCE_STATEMENT: placement decisions.** Both sources allow the text to sit
  elsewhere in the packet: a jacket, or "with the policy documents". The sink is a carrier choice between a static
  resource and the template, so the first route is human review.
- **AOB_ASSIGNMENT_PROHIBITION: form filing.** It's labelled `document_applicability`. If that label were ever routed,
  Document Selection would gate a document on it. It is really policy-form language, bound to the form edition for
  policies issued on or after 2023-01-01.

No approved declarations rule is actually underwriting. The underwriting rows (for example "no nonrenewal solely
because of roof age under 15 years", 627.7011(5)) were triaged out and are routed nowhere, which is correct for the
document system. They are the only evidence that the underwriting class and sink are clean, and they are.

### Genuinely ambiguous

These have a real alternative classification, not just a missing label:

- **AOB:** content vs form version.
- **Binder:** generated document vs delivery process.
- **Agent signature:** workflow only, or also a printed countersignature block.
- **Phone number and law and ordinance:** static insert vs template.
- **Roof deductible:** whether the (e)1 page behind the declarations is a template page or a separately selected
  resource.
- **Sinkhole statement:** the statute says "inform policyholders"; the declarations placement comes from the
  checklist, not the statute.
- **Legislative discounts:** date window as a template conditional vs a dated resource version.

For all of these the right first sink is `human_review`. The decomposition absorbs the ambiguity rather than hiding it.

## NFIP loan-closing effective time, modelled by hand

Source: NFIP Flood Insurance Manual, October 2025 edition (FEMA). Local file
`fema_rsl_national-flood-insurance-manual_06032025.pdf`; the cover reads "October 2025".

- **Section 2, "Before You Start", C. Loan Exception (No Waiting Period).** Printed page 2 • 17 (PDF page 28): "If the
  effective date is the date and time of the loan closing, the declarations page should state the effective date and
  specify that the coverage is effective 'at the time of loan closing' (versus 12:01 a.m.)."
- **Appendix I: Policyholder Communications, E. Requirements, Table 2: Declarations Page Requirements, row ID 2
  "Policy Term".** Printed page I • 12 (PDF page 333). Marked "Always shown": "For loan exception policies, indicate
  the Policy Effective Time as 'at the time of loan closing.' For non-loan exception policies, indicate the Policy
  Effective Time as '12:01am.' For all policies indicate the Policy Expiration Time as '12:01am.'"
- **Legal basis for the loan exception itself.** Footnote 13, printed page 2 • 16: 42 USC 4013(c)(2)(C); 44 CFR §
  61.11(b).

The passage is two rules, not one:

1. **Effective date and time determination: calculation → data_snapshot.** Table 10 decides whether the loan
   exception applies (application and full premium received within the window, and so on). This happens upstream of
   any document. Obtaining the actual closing time "only if a loss occurred on the loan closing date" is claims
   workflow and out of scope.
2. **Declarations wording: content → template, with the snapshot supplying the basis.** The Policy Effective Time
   reads "at the time of loan closing" or "12:01am"; the expiration time is always 12:01am. Obligations:
   `content_presence`, `value_correctness`, `provenance_traceability`.

Written in today's `RegulatoryRule` shape (full object in the JSON), each of the brief's tests comes out as follows:

- **Non-Florida source: partial.** `RuleSource.jurisdiction` is free text, so "US" fits. `RegulatoryRuleSet.jurisdiction`
  is a single value, so this rule can't sit in the Florida set.
- **Manual rather than checklist/statute pair: partial.**
  - No `source_type` fits: the values are regulator_checklist, statute_or_rule, carrier_rulebook, legacy_export and
    policy_document.
  - `source_row` is named for checklists but holds "Appendix I, Table 2, row ID 2" without strain.
  - `StatuteEvidence` works for the federal citations.
  - There is nowhere to record the source edition, which matters here: "October 2025" vs April 2024.
- **Conditional applicability: a vocabulary gap, not a schema gap.** `applies_when` is an open dictionary. The
  predicate registry needs `effective_time_basis`, which is a generic policy fact rather than an NFIP-only one, plus a
  flood product family.
- **Document-content consequence: partial.** Target `declarations` with kind `field_value` works. `fields` must be
  semantic keys of one DocumentModel, and no model has an effective time. The natural reference is the concept
  `policy_period` with qualifier `facet: effective_time`.
- **Provenance: fits, as prose.** There are two locations in one manual plus a separate legal basis, but
  `source_location` is a single string.
- **Human review: fits.** `requires_human_review`, `approved` and `reviewer_note` are enough.
- **Source-specific fields needed: none.** Every gap is either a vocabulary value or a field that every source would
  use.

Strength is also uneven: the narrative says "should", while the table says "indicate" and "Always shown".
`RegulatoryRule` has no strength field (`SelectionRule` does). I recorded this rather than proposing it as part of the
minimum change.

## Answers

### 1. Can one schema describe all classes without conflating them?

Yes, if three things that are fused today are pulled apart: the class, the routes (sink plus validation obligations),
and approval per route.

- **Fused today:** `rule_type` is a single label, while 6 of the 17 rules carry obligations that different owners must
  satisfy: hurricane deductible display, roof deductible display, legislative discounts, hurricane premium split,
  the AOP offer and notice, and AOB. Routing is implied by the target document, and approval is global.
- **What the evidence showed about the proposed decomposition:**
  - Most rules are content or presentation, and those two usually share a sink. That pair is not where conflation
    hurts.
  - Conflation hurts at content vs calculation (three rules) and content vs placement decision (two).
  - `form_version_or_effective_date` was never a primary class. It showed up three times as a secondary aspect: the
    AOB form edition, the AOP notice form, and the legislative discount window. The NFIP source edition is the same
    idea applied to the source rather than the policy. It may work better as dates on a route or resource than as a
    class; keep it as a class for now, with thin evidence.
  - Requirements to offer something have no sink. The closest class, `eligibility_underwriting`, doesn't fit: offering
    a deductible option is product design, not risk acceptance. Add a `product_offering` class with a
    `product_configuration` sink, or treat these as `human_review` until the product team decides.
  - The underwriting rows are clean examples of `eligibility_underwriting` → `underwriting`, but none of them is
    structured today.

### 2. What is the minimum additive schema change?

All optional and defaulted, so existing JSON loads unchanged and current routing is untouched when the new fields are
absent:

1. `RegulatoryRule.rule_class`: the class vocabulary above. `rule_type` stays as a legacy label. With `rule_class`, the
   extractor's "prescribed_statement" output maps to `content_requirement` without the current fallback.
2. `RegulatoryRule.routes: list[RuleRoute]`, where `RuleRoute` has:
   - `sink`
   - `validation_obligations`
   - an optional target document
   - `approved`
   - a note

   One rule can then send its calculation half to the snapshot and its display half to the template, each approved by
   a different owner. With no routes, today's implied declarations route applies, and the legacy `approved` means
   approval of that route.
3. `Requirement.concept_refs`: `concept_id` plus qualifiers, reusing the concept layer. Rules can then name data
   without borrowing one DocumentModel's keys. The application rule and the NFIP rule both need this.
4. `RuleSource.edition`: every source has one. It is distinct from `applies_when` effective dates, which bound
   policies, not sources.
5. Vocabulary additions, not new fields:
   - `source_type`: `program_manual`
   - `TargetDocument`: `binder`, `policy_packet`
   - predicate registry: `effective_time_basis` and a flood product family, with each predicate tagged as
     product-level or policy-level (code, not schema)

Not proposed as minimum:

- a formula or calculation representation: the route to `data_snapshot` with `calculation_correctness` and a named
  owner is enough, and the formula belongs to rating
- `strength`
- `depends_on` between rules
- expected item labels: needed before `value_correctness` works for the legislative discounts; flagged for later

### 3. Which Florida rules are routed to the wrong conceptual sink?

- **Misrouted:** AOB (the label would send it to selection; it's form filing and edition binding), binder (target is
  `policy_form`; really delivery at bind), phone number (presumes declarations; placement is a carrier decision).
- **Unrouted:** AOP offer and notice (belongs to selection plus product configuration), law and ordinance (approved,
  validated nowhere).
- **Partially routed**, where the calculation half has no sink: hurricane deductible display, roof deductible display,
  legislative discounts, hurricane premium split.
- **Mislabeled but reaching the right sink:** agent signature, and the three prescribed statements (flood exclusion,
  hurricane deductible, sinkhole exclusion).

### 4. Which classes can AI draft automatically, and which need explicit human approval before influencing generated artifacts?

AI can draft every class: the text, the classification, the proposed sink and obligations, and provenance. What
differs is what a draft may do before a person approves it.

- **May act on a draft without approval:**
  - `workflow_review`: its sink is a review queue.
  - Any class used as REVIEW-level validation evidence (never FAIL, never shaping output). This is how traceability
    already treats unapproved rules.
- **Needs explicit approval of the route, by the owner of its sink, before influencing any generated artifact:**
  - `content_requirement`: carrier legal, for prescribed text. The existing "never auto-insert regulatory language"
    principle stands.
  - `presentation_requirement`: forms owner. Presentation hints are already gated on `approved`.
  - `document_applicability` and `form_version_or_effective_date`: product and forms owners.
  - `calculation_requirement`: rating/actuarial. AI should not author values into the snapshot.
  - `eligibility_underwriting`: underwriting. It is outside the document system in any case.
  - Offer requirements: product owner.
- **The sink assignment needs approval too.** The phone number and law and ordinance rules show that choosing the sink
  is itself a carrier decision. An AI-proposed route should start as `human_review` whenever its source allows more
  than one placement.

### 5. Does the synthetic Document Selection rulebook contain logic that belongs elsewhere?

Yes: two of its three rules.

- **sr2** ("use the Florida declarations resource for FL") is a resource binding, i.e. resource-manifest
  jurisdictions, not a generate rule. The review note already says so.
- **cr1** (Water Backup row) is row-level content, satisfied by data shape (rows come from the policy's elements). It
  sits in the selection `RuleSet` and is checked by `evaluate.py`, but no Document Selection plugin would run it.

Only sr1 is selection logic. The broken-rulebook conflict test still holds without sr2, because the added "do not
generate for FL" line conflicts with sr1.

The reverse gap matters more: rules that belong in selection aren't there. That includes:

- the AOP notice cadence
- the binder/ID document at bind
- the roof page behind the declarations
- the law and ordinance insert, if the carrier makes it a static insert

## What this audit did not do

- **No implementation or schema changes.** `rule_class`, `routes` and `concept_refs` are proposals only.
- **Not a legal reading.** Classifications are an implementation lead's reading of the statute excerpts and checklist
  text.
- **Unstructured rows were only coverage-noted.** The 19 relevant triaged rows without a rule are listed in the JSON
  with their checklist text and a one-line classification. They were not structured into rules.
- **The NFIP rule is a representation test, not NFIP support.**
